Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Richmond

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.
