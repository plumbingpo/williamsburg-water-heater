When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
