Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Williamsburg, VA, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we check the expansion tank's pressure, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in Williamsburg, call (757) 290-7408.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest is harder than online videos make it seem. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not a place to guess.

Our trucks are stocked for the usual surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, point to future problems, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
