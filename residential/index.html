The Water Heater Team Homeowners Rely On

A busy household runs through a surprising amount of hot water every day, so a struggling water heater disrupts the whole routine, from morning showers to evening laundry. Left alone, a sediment-choked tank or a weeping relief valve can turn a nuisance into a flooded utility room. When that happens, you need a team that shows up fast, diagnoses the fault accurately, and fixes it right the first time. That's the standard Williamsburg Water Heater holds itself to.

From heating elements and thermostats to thermocouples, gas valves, and anode rods, our team handles every residential water heater repair with skill and care.

  • Gas and electric water heater service

No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

No homeowner likes a surprise on the invoice. So Williamsburg Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a complete diagnostic that looks at the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the elements or burner, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is nearly always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap with water on the floor. Our technicians know how local water conditions affect tank life, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Williamsburg Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Right-Sized Hot Water

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending a new heater, Williamsburg Water Heater works through the details that determine your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, showers that run back to back, laundry and dishwasher timing, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a unit with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to handle your morning rush without paying to heat water you'll never use.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Williamsburg

Homes around Williamsburg run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Williamsburg Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
