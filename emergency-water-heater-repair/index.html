How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Heater Repair?

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. You get that answer before any work begins.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
