Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Newport News

Many premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

In Newport News, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
